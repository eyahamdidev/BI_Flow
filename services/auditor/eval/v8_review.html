How the KPI explanation works

A note for friends reviewing the auditor. 29 October 2026.

A KPI here is one business number, such as revenue or the number of orders. The auditor's job is to explain that number in a short paragraph. The model reads the logged facts, chooses which ones a reader needs, and writes the paragraph. A checker then reads that paragraph and rejects it when a number, a direction, or a named place does not match the file.

1. Where the facts come from

The facts are not invented by the model. They come from the project files for that KPI: the name, the two months being compared, the size of the change, each state's contribution, the written definition, the time grain, the source tables, and the cleaning steps. The model sees that list with the values filled in. The list stays in file order. Nothing is pre-ranked.

When the list has more than five facts, the model must keep exactly five and leave the rest out. It must also write the paragraph itself, in three to five sentences. Every fact has to be placed once: either kept or left out. A kept fact has to appear in the paragraph. A fact it left out must not appear.

2. One explanation, end to end

This is the first test sheet, Revenue for January 2017 compared with December 2016. The file offered ten facts:

  • Revenue
  • 2017-01
  • 2016-12
  • Total payments received for delivered orders
  • month
  • 1000 BRL
  • SP 600 BRL
  • RJ 400 BRL
  • raw.orders, raw.order_payments
  • parse_dates, drop_exact_duplicates

The model kept five:

  • Revenue
  • 2017-01
  • 2016-12
  • 1000 BRL
  • SP 600 BRL

It left these out:

  • Total payments received for delivered orders
  • month
  • RJ 400 BRL
  • raw.orders, raw.order_payments
  • parse_dates, drop_exact_duplicates

It wrote:

Revenue went up by 1000 BRL between 2016-12 and 2017-01. The largest contribution came from SP with 600 BRL. The current period is 2017-01.

That answer was accepted on the first try. The checker pulled the numbers 1000, 2016, 12, 2017, 01, and 600 out of the paragraph. Each one is in the file. "Went up" matches a positive change of 1000. "Largest contribution came from SP" matches the file, because SP at 600 is bigger than RJ at 400. RJ, the definition, the word month, the table names, and the cleaning steps do not appear. Revenue, both months, 1000, and SP do appear. There is no "because" and no word such as "important" or "strong".

The checker is an extractor. It pulls numbers, direction words, and names out of the paragraph with patterns and compares them with the file. It does not ask a second model whether the paragraph sounds true, and it does not parse the grammar of the sentence.

3. When the checker rejects a draft

The error is sent back to the model, and the model rewrites. It gets two rewrites. If the third answer is still wrong, Python writes a backup paragraph and the screen says the model was rejected. On this test the backup was never used.

Five sheets were rejected at least once, then accepted: ex07 (2 answers), ex09 (2 answers), ex10 (2 answers), ex12 (2 answers), ex14 (3 answers). The rejected drafts were not saved. Only the accepted paragraph is in this note. ex14 needed two rewrites. The other four needed one.

4. What was tested

The test is one run of the 20 explanation sheets already in the project. There are five KPIs (revenue, orders, average order value, late-delivery rate, average review score) and four pairs of months. The model is openai/gpt-oss-120b. The prompt is explain version 8. Calls were one at a time, with a 2 second pause between sheets. That pause is not included in the times below.

The amounts on these sheets are a test ladder: 1000, 2000, 3000, and so on, split 60/40 across two states. They use the same names, units, tables, and cleaning steps as the project KPIs. They are not a live reading from the warehouse. A late-delivery rate of 13000 in this file is a test number, not a real rate.

Every sheet offered the same ten kinds of facts, so the model had to leave five out. That is the choice this test measures.

6. How to read 15 / 20 and 20 / 20

15 / 20 means the first draft already satisfied the checker. 20 / 20 means that after the allowed rewrites, every published paragraph was the model's writing, and none of those paragraphs contained a number, a direction, or a "largest" place that the file rejects.

This is one run of 20 sheets. The same model can choose different words on another day. A person has not yet marked whether leaving out the definition, or the smaller state, would mislead a reader. That judgement is still open. These figures are the checker's figures.

7. Wording the checker let through

The extractor catches a false number, a false direction, a false "largest" place, a forbidden word, and an exact copy of a fact the model said it dropped. A few accepted sentences still add wording the file does not contain. Three of them are worth seeing before a review.

ex03 · revenue · 2017-11 vs 2017-10

Revenue increased by 3000 BRL between 2017-10 and 2017-11. The largest contribution to this change was from PR, adding 1800 BRL. Revenue tracks the total payments received for delivered orders.

The definition was in the dropped list. The paragraph still says the KPI tracks the total payments received for delivered orders. The letters are lowercase, and the check looks for the exact phrase from the file, so it did not flag this.

ex04 · revenue · 2018-03 vs 2018-02

Revenue increased by 4000 BRL from 2018-02 to 2018-03. The largest contribution came from SC with 2400 BRL. The remaining increase was contributed by other factors.

The last sentence says the rest of the increase was contributed by other factors. The file does not say that. The cause check looks for words such as because and due to. Other factors is not in that list.

ex12 · aov · 2018-03 vs 2018-02

The Average order value in 2018-03 was compared to 2018-02, and the revenue went up by 12000 BRL. The largest contribution came from SC with 7200 BRL. This positive change is reflected in the total delta of 12000 BRL.

The KPI is average order value. The paragraph says the revenue went up. The number 12000 is the logged change. The checker does not flag the extra word revenue.

One more small gap: the check requires the number on a kept change, and it does not require the unit word. On ex16 the model wrote 16000 and 9600 without the word ratio. The numbers are the logged ones.
